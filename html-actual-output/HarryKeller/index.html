About

Practitioner turned designer.

The full cycle — build it, teach it, evaluate it, improve it — is what most instructional design candidates only study.

I've spent the last two years designing and delivering training for a real organization — not as a practicum, but as my job. I started as a classroom teacher at MTC and within a year was promoted to design the curriculum other teachers learn from.

That arc — practitioner to designer to supervisor — defines what I bring: I understand the learner's side of the room because I've been there, and I understand the designer's side because it's now my job.

Every role I've held, from training facility supervisor to team lead to residential mentor, has required me to read a room, communicate clearly, and help people improve. That's the skill I'm building a career on.

What keeps me in this field is the moment when someone's performance visibly improves because the instruction was built right.

🎯
Design from the learner out

Effective training starts by understanding what the learner needs to do, not what the designer wants to say. I build backward from performance goals.

🔄
Iterate on evidence

Performance evaluations and coaching sessions create feedback loops. I treat every cohort as a data point to sharpen the next version of the curriculum.

🗣️
Communication is the delivery mechanism

Content is only as effective as its delivery. Clear, structured facilitation — in front of 5 or 500 — is a core part of the design process.

🌱
Development is relational

Trust between coach and learner accelerates growth. Whether I'm evaluating a teacher or mentoring a struggling resident, relationship quality drives outcomes.
